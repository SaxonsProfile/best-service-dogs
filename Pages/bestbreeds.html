The Most Popular Breed to Use as Service Dogs

The Labrador Retriever is the most popular breed of dog used today as service dogs. The most popular colour type to be used as service dogs are black and yellow.

Labradors are quick learners and can pick up complex commands. Their intelligence helps them retain training and problem-solve in real-life situations—key for tasks like opening doors, retrieving objects, or alerting to medical issues. They're naturally gentle, social, and even-tempered. This makes them reliable in busy or stressful environments like public transit, grocery stores, or hospitals.

Labs are people-oriented and highly motivated to make their handler happy. That willingness to work and strong bond with humans makes training smoother and more successful. They're strong enough to assist with mobility tasks but still have a manageable size—not too small to be limited, and not too large to handle easily.

Labs have been selectively bred for generations as working dogs (originally for retrieving in hunting). Many reputable service dog organizations specifically breed Labs for traits like trainability, health, and temperament.

How to get a Service Dog in Ontario

Under the Accessibility for Ontarians with Disabilities Act (AODA), a service dog is defined as a working animal that supports a person with a disability by performing specific tasks or services that relate directly to their disability. All that is required is for the handler to have a recognized disability and to posses a note from a doctor OR a Regulated Health professional stating the need for the dog.
